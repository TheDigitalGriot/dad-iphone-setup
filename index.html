Your iPhone Setup Guide

Step 1 of 10

Hi Dad!

Welcome to your new iPhone. Let's get it set up together — one step at a time.

What we're doing

We're going to set up your new iPhone and move everything from your Samsung — contacts, messages, photos, and your Google account — over to the iPhone automatically. Apple has a special tool that does this in one shot.

The whole process takes about 30 to 45 minutes. There's no rush. Read each step carefully, and tap Next when you're ready to move on.

What you'll need

1Your new iPhone (turned off for now, plugged into the charger)
2Your Samsung phone (also plugged in, with Wi-Fi turned on)
3Your home Wi-Fi name and password
4Your Google account password (the one you use for Gmail)
5Patience — and maybe a cup of coffee
You can go back

If you ever want to re-read a step, tap the Back button at the bottom. You can also tap any of the dots at the top to jump to a step.

First, prepare your Samsung

Before we touch the iPhone, install a small free app on your Samsung. This app will do the heavy lifting later.

On your Samsung phone

  1. Unlock your Samsung and open the Google Play Store (the colorful triangle icon).
  2. Tap the search bar at the top of the screen.
  3. Type these three words: Move to iOS
  4. Find the app made by Apple Inc. — it's a white icon with a small arrow on it.
  5. Tap the green Install button.
  6. Wait for it to finish downloading. When it's done, do NOT open it yet. We'll come back to it.
Why this app?

Apple built this app specifically to copy your Android data to a new iPhone. It moves your contacts, text messages, photos, videos, calendar events, email accounts, and even some apps — all in one go. It's free and safe.

Keep both phones plugged in

During the transfer (a few steps from now), both phones need to stay on and connected to power. Plug them both in now so they don't run out of battery partway through.

Turn on your iPhone

Now we wake up the iPhone and tell it what language you speak.

Step by step

  1. Press and hold the side button on the right edge of the iPhone for about 3 seconds. Let go when you see the white Apple logo.
  2. Wait about a minute for the phone to start up. You'll see the Apple logo, then a black screen with the word Hello in different languages sliding across.
  3. Swipe your finger up from the bottom of the screen to begin.
What you'll see
"Hello" — "Bonjour" — "Hola" — "你好" — sliding across the bottom of the screen

Pick your language

  1. Tap English (it's usually at the top of the list).
  2. On the next screen, tap United States (or your country, if different).
  3. You'll see a screen called Quick Start with two options. Tap Set Up Manually at the bottom in small blue text.
Why "Set Up Manually"?

Quick Start only works if you have another iPhone or iPad nearby. Since you're coming from Samsung, we'll set it up manually — it's not harder, just a few more taps.

Connect to Wi-Fi

The iPhone needs internet to activate and to move your data from Samsung later.

Step by step

  1. You'll see a screen titled Choose a Wi-Fi Network with a list of nearby networks.
  2. Find your home Wi-Fi name and tap it.
  3. Type your Wi-Fi password carefully (passwords are case-sensitive — capital letters matter).
  4. Tap Join in the top-right corner.
  5. Wait for a blue checkmark to appear next to your network name. This means you're connected.
  6. The iPhone will show Activating iPhone… for about 30 seconds.
  7. When that's done, tap Continue on the Data and Privacy screen that appears next.
Same Wi-Fi for both phones

Make sure your Samsung is also connected to the same Wi-Fi network. They'll need to talk to each other later, and same-Wi-Fi makes it work smoother.

Set up Face ID and a passcode

This is how you'll unlock your iPhone. Face ID uses your face. The passcode is your backup.

Face ID

  1. Tap Continue on the Face ID screen.
  2. Hold the iPhone about 12 inches away from your face (roughly an arm's length).
  3. Position your face inside the circle on screen.
  4. Slowly move your head in a full circle, like you're tracing the outline of a clock face. The phone will guide you with sound.
  5. When it says "First Face ID scan complete," tap Continue and do it one more time.
  6. Tap Continue when both scans are done.
Don't want Face ID?

That's fine. Tap Set Up Later in Settings at the bottom of the Face ID screen. You can always set it up later from the Settings app.

Create a passcode

  1. On the Create a Passcode screen, type a 6-digit code you'll remember.
  2. Avoid obvious ones like 123456, your birth year, or your house number.
  3. Type the same 6 digits again to confirm.
Write the passcode down

If you forget your iPhone passcode, there's no easy way to recover it. Write the 6 digits on a piece of paper and keep it somewhere safe (not on the phone). You'll rarely need to type it — Face ID handles most unlocks.

Move everything from Samsung

This is the most important step. We're going to copy your contacts, messages, photos, and more from your Samsung to your iPhone.

Part A — On your iPhone

  1. You'll see a screen called Transfer Your Apps & Data.
  2. Tap the third option: Move Data from Android.
  3. The iPhone will show a screen with a big 6-digit or 10-digit code. Leave it there — don't touch it.

Part B — On your Samsung

  1. Pick up your Samsung phone.
  2. Open the Move to iOS app you installed earlier (the white icon with the arrow).
  3. Tap Continue, then Agree, then Next on the welcome screens.
  4. When it asks for the code, type in the 6 or 10-digit code showing on your iPhone.
  5. Your Samsung will connect to a temporary Wi-Fi network from the iPhone. This is normal — let it happen.

Part C — Choose what to transfer

  1. On your Samsung, you'll see a list of things to move: Google Account, Messages, Contacts, Calendar, Camera Roll, Photo Albums, Files and Folders, Accessibility Settings, Display Settings, Web Bookmarks, Mail Accounts, WhatsApp Messages.
  2. The boxes are usually all checked by default. Leave them checked.
  3. Tap Continue at the bottom.
  4. Both phones will start the transfer. You'll see a progress bar on the iPhone.
Don't touch either phone

The transfer can take anywhere from 10 minutes to over an hour depending on how many photos you have. Don't lock either screen, don't open other apps, don't unplug them. Just walk away and let them work.

Part D — When the transfer finishes

  1. The iPhone will say Transfer Complete. Tap Continue on iPhone.
  2. On the Samsung, tap Done.
  3. You can put your Samsung down for now. We'll be on the iPhone for the rest of the setup.
What didn't transfer?

Apps from the Google Play Store don't move over directly — they need their iPhone version, which you'll download from the App Store later. Things like WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, your bank app, etc., all have iPhone versions. Don't worry, your account in each app is safe — you just need to log in again.

Set up your Apple ID

An Apple ID is your account for everything Apple — App Store, iCloud, FaceTime, iMessage. You need one to download apps.

Do you already have an Apple ID?

If you've ever bought something from iTunes, used an iPad, or set up an Apple device before — you probably already have one. It would be your email address.

If not, no problem. We'll make a new one in 2 minutes.

If you have one

  1. On the Sign In with Apple ID screen, type your Apple ID email address.
  2. Tap Continue.
  3. Type your Apple ID password.
  4. If it asks for a verification code, check your email or another Apple device for the code.

If you don't have one

  1. Tap Forgot Password or Don't Have an Apple ID? at the bottom.
  2. Tap Create a Free Apple ID.
  3. Enter your date of birth and tap Next.
  4. Enter your first and last name and tap Next.
  5. Choose Use Your Current Email Address and type your Gmail address (the same one you use on your Samsung). Tap Next.
  6. Create a password — at least 8 characters, with one uppercase letter and one number. Write it down somewhere safe.
  7. Add your phone number for security verification. Apple will text or call you a code. Type it in.
  8. Agree to the Terms and Conditions.
Write down your Apple ID and password

This is the most important password on your phone. Without it, you can't download apps, restore from backup, or get into iCloud. Write your email and password on paper and keep it in your wallet or a safe spot.

A few more quick questions

Apple asks about a few features. You can keep this fast — here's what to tap for each.

The screens, in order

  • Make This Your New iPhone — Tap Continue.
  • iCloud Backup — Tap Continue. This automatically backs up your phone every night while it's charging, so you never lose anything.
  • Apple Pay — Tap Set Up Later in Settings. You can add your credit/debit card later when you're more comfortable.
  • Siri — Tap Continue, then say a few phrases like "Hey Siri" when prompted. This teaches the phone your voice. Or tap Set Up Later if you'd rather skip.
  • Screen Time — Tap Continue, then Continue again. This is just usage tracking, not a restriction.
  • App Analytics — Tap Don't Share (your choice — it sends anonymous data to Apple to improve their software).
  • True Tone Display — Tap Continue. Makes the screen colors look natural in different lighting.
  • Appearance — Tap Light or Dark (Light is easier to read, Dark saves battery). Tap Continue.
  • Display Zoom — Pick Larger Text if you want bigger icons and text (recommended). Tap Continue.
  • Welcome to iPhone — Tap Get Started.
If a screen looks different

Apple sometimes adds or removes setup screens. If you see one I didn't mention, read it carefully and tap Continue if you're unsure. You can change every one of these later in the Settings app.

You did it!

Your iPhone is set up and ready to use. Welcome to the Apple side.

What you'll see now

You should be looking at your iPhone's Home Screen — rows of apps on a colorful background. You might see some apps with a small circle around them, slowly filling up. Those are your apps re-downloading from the App Store. Just leave them alone, they'll be ready in a few minutes.

A few small differences from Samsung

  • No app drawer. All your apps live on the Home Screen, in pages you swipe between.
  • Swipe down from top-right for Control Center (Wi-Fi, brightness, flashlight).
  • Swipe down from top-middle for Notifications.
  • Swipe up from the bottom to go back to the Home Screen (instead of a Home button).
  • Hold the side button for Siri, or just say "Hey Siri."

First things to try

Here are five small things to do today to make the iPhone feel like yours.

Today

1
Send me a message. Open the green Messages app, tap the pencil-and-paper icon at the top right, and text me at my number. Just say "hi from my iPhone."
2
Check your email. Open the blue Mail app. Your Gmail should already be there from the transfer. If it asks you to sign in, use your Gmail address and password.
3
Open your photos. Tap the colorful flower icon for Photos. Scroll around — everything from your Samsung should be there.
4
Download one app you use a lot. Open the blue App Store icon. Tap Search at the bottom right. Type the name of an app you use on your Samsung (Facebook, WhatsApp, your bank). Tap Get, sign in with your Apple ID if asked, and it'll install.
5
Adjust the text size. If text feels small, open Settings (gray gear icon) → Display & Brightness → Text Size. Drag the slider to the right to make everything bigger.

When you need help

Call me anytime. There's no question too small. iPhones work differently from Samsung in a few ways, and it takes a couple weeks to feel natural. That's totally normal.

You can also ask Siri for help. Just hold down the side button and say something like "How do I change my wallpaper?" or "Show me my battery percentage." Siri will walk you through it.

One last tip

The iPhone is yours now. Try things. Tap on things you don't recognize. You can't break it. If something goes wrong, you can always restart the phone by holding the side button and the volume-up button together.

Love you, Dad. Enjoy the new phone.
